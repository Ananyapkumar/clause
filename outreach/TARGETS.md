# Who to contact, and how to find them

**Day 21.** The list matters more than the message. A perfect message to the
wrong person converts at zero.

---

## The filter

Contact someone only if you can answer **yes to all four**:

1. **Does their company ship, or is it visibly building, an LLM feature?**
   Not "uses AI" in the marketing copy — an actual feature.
2. **Are they small enough that this person can decide?**
   Under ~200 people. At 2,000, your message goes to a recruiting funnel.
3. **Can I name one specific thing about them or their product?**
   A post, a repo, a launch, a job ad, a feature I have used.
4. **Is there a plausible path to my wedge?**
   Do they have something whose correctness is measurable and consequential?

If any answer is no, skip. There are more companies than you have days.

## Tiers, in priority order

### Tier 1 — Document/data extraction startups (highest fit)

Companies extracting structured data from unstructured documents: invoices,
contracts, medical records, insurance, construction submittals, lab reports,
compliance filings, spec sheets.

**Why:** your project is literally their problem, and the evaluation gap is
their most acute pain — accuracy is the product, so they cannot hand-wave it.

**Find them:** search LinkedIn for "document extraction", "intelligent document
processing", "IDP"; look at YC batches (W24 onward), Product Hunt AI/data
categories, and who is hiring for "AI Engineer" with "extraction" in the
description.

**Who to message:** founding engineer, Head of AI, CTO.

---

### Tier 2 — Your own industry (highest conversion, lowest competition)

Lighting, electrical, construction tech, BIM, specification platforms,
manufacturer product-data teams, wholesaler catalogue teams.

**Why:** you are the only applicant on earth who knows what L80/B10 means and
can also build the system. There is essentially no competition here, and the
conversation starts from shared ground rather than from you proving yourself.

**Find them:** lighting manufacturers with digital/product-data roles;
construction-tech startups; specification platforms; distributors with product
databases; anyone posting about "product data quality" or "catalogue
normalisation".

**Who to message:** Head of Product Data, Digital Transformation lead, CTO,
founder.

**Do not skip this tier because it feels less glamorous than an AI startup.**
It is the tier where you are the obvious choice rather than one of two hundred.

---

### Tier 3 — Small AI consultancies and agencies

10–50 people, building LLM systems for clients.

**Why:** they are perpetually capacity-constrained, they subcontract, and they
hire on demonstrated ability rather than CV. A free scoped piece of work is a
much easier yes for them than for a product company.

**Find them:** LinkedIn "AI consultancy", "AI implementation partner";
who is posting about client LLM projects; agencies with open contractor roles.

---

### Tier 4 — GenAI tech leads posting publicly

People writing about evals, RAG, agents, prompt engineering.

**Why:** they already care about your wedge and will engage on substance. Lower
chance of work, higher chance of a useful relationship, a referral, or a signal
boost.

**Rule for this tier: comment on three posts before connecting.** A connection
request from a stranger is noise. From someone whose comments they recognise, it
is a person.

---

### Tier 5 — Remote-first companies hiring AI engineers

Actual job ads.

**Why:** direct, but the most competitive route and the slowest to reply.
**Do not lead with this.** One or two per week, and always message a human as
well as applying — the application goes into a queue, the message does not.

## The Day 21 task: build 40 names

Not 400. Forty, researched properly.

| Tier | Count |
|---|---|
| 1 — extraction / document AI | 12 |
| 2 — lighting, construction, product data | 12 |
| 3 — AI consultancies | 6 |
| 4 — public GenAI leads | 6 |
| 5 — remote AI roles | 4 |

**Notice tier 2 is equal-largest.** That is deliberate and it is where the
first yes most likely comes from.

For each row in `tracker.csv`, fill in before sending anything:
- name, company, role, LinkedIn URL
- **the specific thing you will reference** — if this cell is empty, do not send

That last column is the whole system. Ten minutes of research per person.
Four hours gets you the list.

## Cadence, from Day 22

| | |
|---|---|
| Connection requests | 10 per day, weekdays |
| Stage-2 messages | to everyone who accepted 2–4 days ago |
| Comments on posts | 3 per day, Tier 4 people, before connecting |
| Follow-ups | one per contact, 7–10 days after Stage 2, then stop |
| Review | Sundays — reply rate by tier, and drop what is not working |

Roughly 45–60 minutes a day. It runs alongside the build work; it does not
replace it.

---

## The thing that will actually determine whether this works

Not the templates. **Whether you send them.**

The most common failure here is spending three weeks perfecting a message and
sending eleven of them. Fifty adequate messages beat five excellent ones,
because the variable you cannot control — whether that person happens to have a
problem this week — only resolves with volume.

Send ten a day. Improve the message from the replies, not from imagining them.
